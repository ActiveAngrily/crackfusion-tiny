# CNN–Transformer hybrids for crack detection and segmentation

**Tags:** `crack-segmentation` `crack-detection` `CNN-Transformer` `dual-encoder` `parallel-branch` `local-global-fusion` `concrete` `pavement` `bridge` `dam` `tunnel` `EfficientCrackNet` `MobileViT`

**Scope:** crack localization and pixel-level segmentation in civil infrastructure, especially concrete, pavement, bridges, dams, tunnels, and masonry. Search current to **6 October 2026**.

## Main findings

- CNN–Transformer crack segmentation is an established research direction. The core set in this note contains **22 distinct crack-specific published models/papers (2023–2026)** that meet the inclusion rule below. Two near-matches are also listed as adjacent work but are excluded from that count. This is a scoped search result, not an exhaustive bibliometric census.
- The recurring motivation is the same local/global complementarity: convolution is used to preserve local texture, edges, and thin crack detail; attention is used to capture longer-range context. The papers differ in where and how they fuse these features, how they reconstruct masks, and what domain/data they target.
- Most of these works solve **segmentation**: they predict a crack mask at pixel level. That differs from our current SDNET2018 **image-level binary classification** task, where the output is one crack/no-crack score for a tile. Segmentation papers are relevant architecture precedents, but their mask metrics and reported scores are not direct baselines for our classifier.
- CrackFusion-Tiny shares the established local-CNN/global-Transformer idea. Its distinguishing design is a compact classifier: it reuses a shared stem, makes one 14×14 local feature map and one 14×14 Transformer feature map, concatenates them, fuses them once with a 1×1 convolution, then globally pools to one logit. The current implementation has **284,337 trainable parameters** ([source](../../notebooks/03_crackfusion_train.ipynb)). Many segmentation hybrids instead use separate, larger encoders, several fusion points, skip connections, and a pixel decoder.
- Small size alone is not a novel claim: **EfficientCrackNet** reports 0.26M parameters and combines depthwise convolutions with MobileViT for crack segmentation. Its task is still a pixel mask, and its datasets are Crack500, DeepCrack, and GAPs384. This is an especially useful comparison when describing model scale, while keeping the task difference explicit.

## What counts as a hybrid here?

### Inclusion rule for the count

The table marks the exact **22 core papers** as `Core 01` through `Core 22`. A paper is counted when it proposes a crack-specific pixel-segmentation network with a separately identifiable CNN feature-processing component and Transformer feature-processing component, and explicitly combines their representations in that network. The components may be parallel or staged. Convolution used only inside a Transformer block does not qualify by itself. A detector-based pipeline that combines an object detector and ViT is useful adjacent work but is outside this semantic-segmentation count.

The two rows marked `Adjacent` show the boundary: SwinCrack inserts convolution into a Swin-based segmentation architecture without a distinct CNN feature path; MaskerTransformer is a Mask R-CNN/ViT detection-and-mask pipeline. Neither is included in the 22.

Architectures in the core set fall into several patterns:

1. **Parallel encoders / dual paths:** a CNN and Transformer process features in parallel and fuse at one or more scales. This is closest to the local/global explanation of CrackFusion-Tiny, though these models normally output masks.
2. **Mixed or staged encoder:** CNN blocks extract early/local features and Transformer blocks model deeper/global features, often followed by multi-scale skip fusion and a decoder.
3. **Transformer with convolutional modules:** convolution is inserted into a Transformer architecture to restore local inductive bias. These are related hybrid designs, but do not necessarily have a separate CNN branch.
4. **Broader detection pipelines:** region proposals, scene understanding, or cascaded classification/segmentation combine CNN and Transformer components across stages. They are adjacent rather than direct dual-branch analogues.

## Related but excluded from the core count

The main table marks two adjacent model papers as `Adjacent`; the items below add further context but are not included in the 22-paper core set:

- [SegCrack (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2022.104275) is a Transformer encoder plus feature-pyramid decoder. It is an important Transformer-only crack segmentation baseline, not a CNN–Transformer hybrid.
- [CNN-SegFormer scene-aware crack segmentation (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.istruc.2024.106685) combines/cascades Transformer and CNN processing with scene information for field images, including steel bridges and concrete buildings. It is a broader scene-understanding pipeline.
- SwinCrack and MaskerTransformer are already detailed in the main table and marked `Adjacent`, so they are excluded from the 22-paper count.
- [Automatic real-time crack detection using lightweight deep learning models (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.engappai.2024.109340) compares 14 lightweight CNN, ViT, and hybrid configurations on DeepCrack. It is a useful evaluation study, rather than a new standalone CNN–Transformer crack architecture.
- [The 2025 surface-crack review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.measurement.2025.116908) organizes the wider field by classification, recognition, and segmentation and discusses dataset scarcity and evaluation standardization. It is useful background, not a census of CNN–Transformer hybrids.
- [The 2025 crack-learning-paradigms review/preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10256) surveys generalization, learning regimes, and datasets; it is broader than this architecture-focused note and remains a preprint.

## How this differs from our MobileViT baseline and CrackFusion-Tiny

The central distinction is **where the CNN and Transformer information lives, and what the network must output**.

- **MobileViT** is a family of compact hybrid blocks. A typical MobileViT block uses convolutions for local processing, rearranges local patches into tokens, applies Transformer processing, then folds the result back into a feature map. So it already mixes local convolution and global attention *inside the same feature-processing stages*.
- **CrackFusion-Tiny** has a shared convolutional stem, followed by two branches with different jobs. One branch continues local depthwise-separable convolutions. A second branch tokenizes the shared map and applies two Transformer encoder layers. Their feature maps are concatenated once, compressed with a 1×1 convolution, globally pooled, and sent to one binary output. This explicit local/global branch design is the point of comparison; it is not a standard MobileViT block.
- **Most crack-segmentation hybrids in the table** keep and fuse multiple spatial feature maps, then use decoder/skip connections to produce a pixel mask. They generally require per-pixel ground-truth masks and use IoU/Dice/boundary metrics. Our current classification data and evaluation answer a different question and require different labels/metrics.

Several related architectures make a **small-model** novelty claim especially difficult to defend by itself: EfficientCrackNet (0.26M parameters) and the WACV 2025 paper above, CrackTrNet’s mobile patrol application, and LiteCrackSeg’s explicit edge-device efficiency all predate or are contemporaneous with this work. A defensible contribution must therefore rest on the precise architecture and a rigorous, task-matched evaluation—not on being the first CNN–Transformer crack model or the first compact hybrid.

## What this means for the paper

1. Do not claim that combining CNN and Transformer features for cracks is new. Cite the closest precedents—DTrC-Net, the 2024 dual-path pavement network, DefNet or CCDFormer, and EfficientCrackNet for compactness.
2. Compare MobileViT and other models on the **same image-level binary task, data, split, and training budget**. Segmentation scores from the table are background context, not apples-to-apples results.
3. Describe the model’s potential contribution narrowly: a small shared-stem classifier with explicitly parallel local and global branches, evaluated for binary crack/no-crack recognition. Whether this is novel enough depends on a targeted search of classification papers and, most importantly, on reliable source-separated and cross-surface results.
4. Keep performance claims pending until the project’s planned independent evaluation is run. Model parameters or architecture diagrams alone do not establish better accuracy or generalization.

## Search coverage and limits

Searches were conducted on 6 October 2026 using combinations of `crack segmentation CNN transformer`, `dual encoder`, `dual path`, `local global`, and infrastructure terms for concrete, pavement/road, bridge, dam, tunnel, and masonry. Results were checked against publisher or proceedings pages and, where available, official author/university repositories and code repositories. The targeted window emphasized 2022–2026, with older general crack-segmentation work used only for context.

The **22-paper figure is exactly the number of main-table rows marked `Core 01` through `Core 22`** under the stated inclusion rule; it is not a claim that exactly 22 such papers exist in the literature. Search indexing, terminology differences, non-English proceedings, newly published 2026 work, and hybrids described without “CNN–Transformer” in their titles can hide additional papers. The list also contains varied supervision, datasets, and mask definitions, so reported metrics should not be ranked across rows as if they came from one benchmark. A true exhaustive count would need a documented database-by-database systematic search, deduplication, and inclusion/exclusion screening.
