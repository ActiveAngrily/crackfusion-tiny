# Interpretability, measurement, and task extensions

**Survey date:** 6 October 2026\
**Scope:** explainability and faithfulness, crack localization, physical width measurement, severity labels, and task-specific evaluation. This extends the image-level SDNET2018 classification review; it does not change the present task definition.

## Current task and evidence boundary

The current model predicts one binary label per 224×224 tile (crack / no crack). SDNET2018 supplies image-level labels and no pixel masks, calibrated physical widths, severity grades, structural metadata, or per-tile source-photo mapping in the reviewed project archive. The project review records a 3,600-image development subset and an unresolved full tile→parent-photo map. Consequently, a heatmap can only be an explanation/localization diagnostic for the classifier; it is not a ground-truth crack mask, measurement, or diagnosis. Do not infer severity, safety, or repair priority from binary classification or a visually plausible saliency overlay.

Three output claims must stay distinct:

| Claim | Required target/evidence | Suitable evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| Image-level explanation | Does a map identify image regions that affect this model's crack score? | Model/data randomization sanity checks; deletion/insertion or controlled occlusion; stability and counterfactual checks. Human plausibility is supplementary. |
| Pixel localization | Which pixels are crack? | Independently annotated crack masks and held-out-source segmentation evaluation: precision/recall, Dice/IoU, boundary quality, thin-structure continuity, per-image results. Classification labels cannot validate this. |
| Physical width/severity/structural state | How wide is a crack in physical units, or what condition/risk does it indicate? | Calibrated image geometry or depth/stereo, traceable physical reference measurements, explicit severity criteria, independent structure/site test, uncertainty and engineering validation. A binary label or mask alone cannot supply this. |

## Explanation methods and what they establish

| Method/reference | What it computes | Appropriate use and limitation here |
|---|---|---|
| Grad-CAM, Selvaraju et al. (2017), [primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02391) | Gradient-weighted coarse localization from a target class score and a convolutional feature layer. | A lightweight visual diagnostic for positive and negative decisions; spatial resolution is coarse and the map is class-discriminative evidence, not a segmentation prediction. Select the target score/layer and interpolation explicitly. |
| LIME, Ribeiro et al. (2016), [KDD paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2939672.2939778); SHAP, Lundberg & Lee (2017), [NeurIPS paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2017/hash/8a20a8621978632d76c43dfd28b67767-Abstract.html) | Local surrogate or Shapley-inspired feature attribution under a chosen feature representation and perturbation/background distribution. | Image superpixels can yield local attribution hypotheses, but results depend on segmentation, masking baseline, sample budget, and distribution shift from perturbed images. They do not produce crack masks or causal explanations automatically. Prefer one method for a focused diagnostic; comparing many methods without a question adds little. |
| Attention weights / rollout | Summarize selected attention operations. | Useful for inspecting the Transformer branch's internal routing, but attention values alone do not establish feature importance or output causality. Jain & Wallace's [primary study](https://aclanthology.org/N19-1357/) finds materially different attention distributions can yield similar predictions in tested NLP settings; this is a caution, not direct crack-image validation. |
| Saliency faithfulness checks, Adebayo et al. (2018), [NeurIPS primary paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/hash/294a8ed24b1ad22ec2e7efea049b8737-Abstract.html) | Tests whether explanations change when learned parameters or labels are randomized. | Run at least a model-parameter randomization check (and ideally label randomization) before describing maps as model-specific explanations. Attractive edge-like heatmaps can be insensitive to what the model learned. |

For image-level explanations, define faithfulness narrowly as sensitivity of the model output to the features the method ranks. On a held-out, source-aware sample, compare ranked salient-region removal with random-region and low-saliency removal controls, while keeping the prediction score/logit as the response. Use more than one plausible perturbation baseline because black rectangles, blur, or inpainting can themselves create out-of-distribution cues. Report curves and uncertainty across images, not selected examples alone. Also check whether attribution changes under small label-preserving photometric/crop perturbations, and whether the model relies on background, borders, markings, or texture cues. These checks support a claim about this model's behavior under the chosen interventions; they do not prove a human causal rationale.

Useful explanation evidence for this application would combine: (1) maps that pass model-randomization checks; (2) greater score change for removing top-ranked regions than matched random/low-ranked regions across perturbation choices; (3) consistency across seeds and source-photo-held-out examples; and (4) expert-confirmed crack alignment on a separately annotated subset. Only the first three support model-faithfulness claims; expert alignment supports task relevance, not faithfulness by itself. For a segmentation model, assess the predicted mask directly against masks rather than presenting Grad-CAM as localization validation.

## Localization, width, and severity: primary crack evidence

| Source | Task/data/evaluation evidence | Access and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Dorafshan, Thomas & Maguire, “SDNET2018: A dataset for non-contact crack detection and localization,” [primary article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2018.11.015) | 56,092 labeled tiles from 230 source photos; labels are crack/non-crack at tile level. | Full open article read; it establishes acquisition/count context, not per-pixel masks, width, or a complete tile-parent map. |
| “A pre-failure narrow concrete cracks dataset for engineering structures damage classification and segmentation,” [Scientific Data primary paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-023-02839-z) | Dataset explicitly includes image classification and manually annotated pixel segmentation; method describes pixel labels and annotation decisions for narrow crack traces. | Primary full-text/PDF search result inspected; no archive download or label audit in this pass. Candidate for pixel localization, subject to license, split, and annotation consistency audit. |
| Liu et al., “Two-Stream Boundary-Aware Neural Network for Concrete Crack Segmentation and Quantification” (2023), [primary article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1155/2023/3301106) | Segments boundaries and estimates physical width via pixel width plus a pixel/mm calibration ratio; includes smartphone field images and observer width comparison. | Primary full text open/read for quantification section. Physical scale calibration is a required part of conversion; image pixels alone are not millimetres. |
| Bae & An, “Computer vision-based statistical crack quantification for concrete structures” (2023), [primary article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.measurement.2023.112632) | Segmentation, branch separation, distance-transform width/length estimates; reports field comparison average differences of 18.07% (width) and −26.28% (length), and statistical width intervals. | Publisher abstract/highlights inspected; full article was paywalled. Treat reported figures as abstract evidence, not independently reproduced results. |
| Sreedhara et al., “Practical Application of Digital Image Processing in Measuring Concrete Crack Widths in Field Studies” (2023), [ASCE primary record](https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)SC.1943-5576.0000735) | Two field studies apply digital image processing to concrete crack width measurement. | Publisher abstract/record inspected; full text not retrieved. |
| “Pixel-level crack segmentation and quantification enabled by multi-modality cross-fusion of RGB and depth images” (2025), [primary publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2025.141961) | RGB-D segmentation plus width quantification; publisher abstract reports absolute error below 0.05 mm for tested thin cracks. | Primary publisher abstract/index material inspected; full text not read. Result is setup-specific; do not transfer its precision to monocular SDNET tiles. |
| Yeganehfallah et al., “Geometry-guided semantic segmentation for automated crack width quantification” (2026), [primary publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2026.107071) | Segmentation plus geometric width estimation; reports validation on sections 0.38–6.21 mm and 3.41% mean error, with a stated pixel-resolution limit near 0.084 mm. | Publisher abstract/index inspected; full methods not retrieved. Calibration/resolution conditions govern these numbers. |
| Shi & Luo, “Deep learning for automated detection and classification of crack severity” (2025), [primary article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2025.140793) | Four image labels: intact, minor, moderate, severe; 8,000 images sourced from CCIC and SDNET2018. | Publisher abstract/dataset excerpts inspected; label thresholds, source grouping, and exact split unresolved. The accessible evidence supports image-grade classification precedent, not calibrated severity or structural diagnosis. |

Crack masks can support geometry in pixels, but absolute width needs image scale and imaging geometry: known calibration target or calibrated camera plus distance/plane assumptions, or depth/stereo. Perspective, obliquity, resolution, focus, blur, thresholding, skeleton branches, and crack width variation all affect the result. Validate against a traceable physical gauge/observer on the same locations; report bias and error distribution (e.g. MAE, signed error, limits/intervals), repeatability, measurable-width range, and calibration conditions. A single mean error can hide unacceptable tails. Structural severity additionally depends on crack type/location, loading, material, exposure and engineering criteria; visual width class alone is not a diagnosis.

## Concrete follow-up experiments, in order

1. **No-label explanation audit on the current binary classifier.** Use a fixed held-out set with verified provenance where available; produce Grad-CAM for both classes and representative true/false positives/negatives. Run parameter-randomization sanity checks and controlled deletion/occlusion with random and low-importance controls under multiple baselines. Save per-image maps, logits and curves. Do not call maps masks.
2. **Small expert localization pilot.** Select images by surface, lighting, texture and prediction outcome, then annotate crack pixels (or crack centerlines if mask boundaries are ambiguous) with a written convention and second-reader adjudication. Split by parent photo/site before tuning; report inter-annotator agreement and segmentation metrics. This tests whether classifier saliency aligns with annotated cracks and whether a separate segmentation model is justified.
3. **Separate segmentation benchmark.** Only after suitable masks are audited, train/evaluate a mask-output model with source-photo grouping and an external/site holdout. Measure Dice/IoU plus boundary and continuity errors; preserve full-resolution scoring for thin traces. Do not compare its pixel metrics with classifier tile F1 as if they measured the same capability.
4. **Width metrology study.** Capture a new calibrated image set with range of physical widths, viewing angles, materials and lighting; record scale/depth and co-located crack-width-comparator measurements. Test segmentation-to-width algorithms on a sealed site-level test, stratify by width and angle, and quantify measurement uncertainty/repeatability. Monocular SDNET resizing is insufficient for mm calibration.
5. **Severity only with an operational label protocol.** Define classes with engineering stakeholders and a reference standard (measured width plus relevant crack/context attributes); keep image-derived grade distinct from structural condition. Collect multiple structures/sites and evaluate ordinal errors, per-class recall, calibration, and site-held-out transfer. Avoid reusing unverified “minor/moderate/severe” labels as ground truth for risk.

For all extensions, group correlated crops by original photo and higher-level structure/site when metadata allows; split before augmentation; retain natural test prevalence; freeze thresholds on validation; report per-group counts and uncertainty at the acquisition-cluster level. Existing project review documents the missing SDNET parent map, so first resolve provenance before claiming source-photo-independent explanation or localization evaluation.
