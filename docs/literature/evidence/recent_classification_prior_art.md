# Recent direct crack-classification prior art

**Cutoff:** 6 October 2026. Target is image-level crack presence, type, or severity classification. Detection and segmentation are included only where needed to distinguish task boundaries. This is a focused extension of [Review/01](crack_classification.md), not an exhaustive search.

## Direct comparison and novelty boundary

The most consequential finding is EfficientCrackFusion: a pre-cutoff, named preprint that already has two parallel image encoders, distinct local and global roles, and explicit cross-attention fusion for binary crack classification. Thus the project cannot claim the first parallel CNN–Transformer classifier for cracks, the first local/global split, or the first adaptive cross-attention. Its paper reports a hash/visual-cluster split, but that does not demonstrate independent parent structures, acquisition sites, or held-out SDNET surfaces; moreover, model-size claims conflict with the stated ViT-Base backbone. The right comparison is architecture and evidence protocol, not its headline score.

CrackNeXt is staged rather than parallel and its cutoff date remains unsettled. Attention-enhanced ConvNeXt is a strong single-backbone attention comparator; its record identifies two different task/data label setups (METU binary, SDNET multiclass), not one common score. DBD-KD’s dual branches describe distillation supervision, not confirmed dual-path inference. Severity and MobileViT papers address four-level severity or general defect classes, which are not interchangeable with binary crack presence. None of their visible records establishes physical width thresholds or structural-risk labels.

The defensible project contribution, if experiments support it, is narrower: a specified small topology and measured fusion effect under capacity-matched controls, using verified SDNET parent-photo groups and held-out-surface evaluation. That is a protocol- and implementation-specific contribution, not an architectural “first.” Do not compare accuracy/F1 across the cited papers as a leaderboard: class definitions, image sources, split units, class balance, pretraining, and hardware differ or remain unknown.
