# Image-level crack classification: evidence review

**Scope:** Image/patch-level crack presence, type, and severity classification, especially concrete, SDNET2018, and CNN–Transformer classifiers. Localization and pixel masks are outside the primary evidence set.\
**Search date:** 6 October 2026.\
**Coverage:** Targeted primary-source search, not an exhaustive census. Full-text availability varied. This ledger distinguishes full-text reading from abstract/metadata-only screening.

## Search and screening record

Searches combined: `concrete crack image classification`, `SDNET2018 crack classification`, `CNN Transformer concrete crack classification`, `crack severity image classification`, `MobileViT crack`, `Swin crack classification`, and exact-title/DOI searches. Publisher pages and publisher-hosted PDFs were preferred; the SDNET2018 data paper and relevant open articles were checked. Included image/patch class outputs: binary crack presence, crack type, or severity. Detection and segmentation papers were excluded unless needed to clarify task boundaries. Older classifiers were retained as task baselines.

Search log: 6 October 2026, targeted searches across publisher records and open full texts (Elsevier, Springer Nature, MDPI, Frontiers, SAGE, PMC, arXiv). Direct publisher access was rate-limited for some sources; searchable publisher text/PDF results were used to locate relevant sections and are identified as partial where applicable. Citation chaining, subscription proceedings, non-English literature, and papers not indexed by the searches remain coverage gaps. “Read everything” cannot be established by this targeted search; no exhaustive-search claim is made.

## Detailed synthesis

### SDNET2018 and the unit of evaluation

SDNET2018 is a patch-classification resource, not 56,092 independent scenes: its 56,092 256×256 tiles derive from 230 parent photographs. The dataset paper presents an AlexNet baseline and documents substantial variation in crack width, shadows, roughness, edges, scaling, holes, and debris. The effective independent source count is far below the tile count. Randomly partitioning tiles without retaining parent IDs risks sharing capture conditions and nearby surface content between train and test. This is a methodological risk, not proof that every prior score is inflated.

The 2018 Kim et al. study predates SDNET2018 and highlights the central binary classification challenge: distinguish cracks from confusing background patterns. SDNET2018 made this task widely benchmarkable but did not remove the need to account for source-photo dependence. In this project, a source-photo-separated split is part of the research question; a tile-level split remains preliminary as stated in [the experimental protocol](../../planning/experimental_protocol.md).

### What prior hybrid classifiers establish

CSW-S (2024) and CCTNet (2024) establish that Transformer-based and CNN–Transformer concrete crack classifiers predate this study. CSW-S is a modified Swin with convolutional/residual operations; CCTNet serially feeds CNN representations into attention. The 2025 Swin/EFRB model adds convolution within a hierarchical Transformer. Sait et al. (2026) stages MobileNetV3 and LeViT and adds Linformer/gated fusion. DBD-KD (2026) adds frequency/spatial branches for distillation, though its full student inference graph is not verified in accessible text. These are relevant precedents, but their scores and compute are not comparable without matching datasets, splits, labels, training, and hardware.

The surveyed papers do not establish CrackFusion-Tiny’s exact compact layout: a shared stem followed by explicit parallel local-CNN/global-attention paths and one concat-plus-1×1 fusion. This supports describing a specific architecture and testing it under a stronger split protocol. It does not support claiming the general CNN–Transformer idea or local/global feature fusion is new. Existing crack segmentation hybrids also use parallel feature paths, although segmentation is a distinct task.

Most directly relevant prior results remain limited by unclear or tile-level partitioning. CCTNet’s high fold scores, CSW-S’s high accuracy, and the 2026 AttXNet results answer different questions from a source-photo-held-out test. AttXNet is especially useful as a recent SDNET bridge-deck deployment comparator, but its v2 openly identifies image-level correlation as a limitation and its ground-station batch FPS should not be presented as onboard UAV speed.

### Severity and related classifiers

Shi and Luo provide a direct four-label severity-classification precedent using CCIC and SDNET2018. Those labels are not interchangeable with binary presence labels. Their abstract does not establish measured crack-width cutoffs or calibrated structural risk; verify the full label protocol before describing the task as engineering severity assessment. Since SDNET2018 is reused, it is not an independent external test collection for this project.

Özdemir et al. and Parvathy & Sivasankar are relevant newer classification candidates. The first is attention-enhanced ConvNeXt, not a Transformer fusion model; the second uses MobileViT and describes reliability-oriented splitting, but requires primary-text verification. Keep their source-status limitations visible in any manuscript use.

CrackNeXt requires a cutoff-date note. The suggested DOI `10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2026.144490` was not verified. Search located the direct-classification paper *CrackNeXt: An efficient local-to-context hybrid network for concrete surface crack classification in construction material inspection*, DOI `10.1016/j.cscm.2026.e06223`. Its [publisher issue listing](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214509526004742) gives *Case Studies in Construction Materials*, volume 25, December 2026, while a [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/406423686_CrackNeXt_An_Efficient_Local-to-Context_Hybrid_Network_for_Concrete_Surface_Crack_Classification_in_Construction_Material_Inspection) says June 2026. Publisher full chronology was inaccessible in this survey, so its status on 6 October 2026 is ambiguous. Do not silently omit it from novelty monitoring or treat its publication-before-cutoff status as settled. Its abstract describes ConvFormer local stages followed by Swin context stages; METU binary classification and SDNET2018 six-class classification; 99.90% METU accuracy and 90.67% accuracy / 90.29% macro-F1 on SDNET2018; 17.22M parameters and 2.66 GMACs. These remain abstract-level author claims pending full-text and date verification.

### Implications for CrackFusion-Tiny

- State the task as **binary concrete crack image classification**, not localization, crack-width measurement, or structural diagnosis.
- Do not claim CNN–Transformer crack classification is novel. The defensible distinction is the specific compact parallel topology plus a source-photo-separated, cross-surface evaluation, if completed.
- Treat verified SDNET2018 parent photographs as the grouping unit. Document how parent IDs were established; do not infer provenance from filenames alone.
- Report positive-class recall/F1 and precision–recall behavior at natural test prevalence, alongside balanced accuracy, specificity, and confusion matrices. Accuracy alone can hide misses on an imbalanced dataset.
- Compare architectures only under matched partitions, preprocessing, training budgets, and seed policy. MobileViT-XS is a pretrained practical reference; scratch-trained matched family baselines answer a different architectural question.
- Separate binary crack-presence claims from severity-classification claims. Severity needs defensible label criteria and independent validation.
- Report latency, memory, and parameter count under common conditions. Ground-station batch throughput is not onboard deployment evidence.
