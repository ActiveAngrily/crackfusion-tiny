# Review literature, evidence quality, and paper position

**Cutoff:** 6 October 2026. This is a focused update to the project’s scoping review, for the proposed compact concrete-crack image classifier. It is not a PRISMA review or a claim to have enumerated every paper in the field.

## Scope and search/screening record

The review question is whether the existing literature leaves a defensible study contribution for a very small image-level concrete-crack classifier with an explicit local-CNN/global-attention split, assessed under source-photo-separated and cross-surface evaluation. The target is binary classification of a crop/image, not pixel-level crack masks, boxes, physical width, severity, or structural diagnosis.

The project corpus was checked against `sources/zotero_snapshot.json` (24 top-level records). The prior synthesis reports full-text reading of the then-current 22 corpus records, with the two interpretability papers added to the methodology evidence base. Recent survey screening was extended using title/abstract and publisher or author-hosted full text where available. Search concepts included “deep learning crack detection review”, “crack classification concrete review”, “crack generalizability datasets”, “scientometric crack detection”, “CNN Transformer crack classification”, and “3DCrack”. Publisher records, DOI pages, conference proceedings, and author preprints were preferred for title, publication status, scope, and date checks. A paper was retained here when it informs crack-image task taxonomy, data/evaluation quality, generalization, or interpretability; segmentation/detection reviews are contextual evidence, not direct classifier comparisons.

The cutoff is applied to the actual publication/version date, not the indexing or crawl date. In particular, Mohammad et al.’s focused Springer review has a version-of-record date of **26 September 2026**, so it is in scope. A Neurocomputing survey surfaced with a publication date of **28 December 2026**; it is post-cutoff and excluded, regardless of search result availability. Zhang et al. (2025) is treated as an arXiv preprint/review plus dataset/benchmark paper, not as a peer-reviewed journal review absent confirmation of a later publication.

The 3DCrack data are high-resolution 3D pavement profiles; they do not answer whether a 284k-parameter RGB binary classifier separates parent photographs or transfers across SDNET surface types. The 2026 reviews make similarly useful broad-gap statements, but must not be used as direct evidence that the proposed model works.

## What the literature establishes—and what it does not

Crack image classification is only one resolution of the task. Classification gives a crack/no-crack label for an image or crop; detection predicts a bounding region; segmentation predicts pixels. Measurement requires calibrated geometry or depth, and diagnosis requires contextual structural/material evidence. Review papers often discuss these tasks together under “crack detection”, but their results and metrics are not interchangeable. A high segmentation IoU or detector mAP does not validate binary crop classification; a classifier cannot localize a crack or estimate physical width.

The broader architectural idea is established. CNNs provide local, translation-biased representations; ViT and later hybrids combine convolutional processing, tokens, attention, and varied fusion schemes. Crack-specific literature has also already combined convolution and Transformers, including CCTNet’s concrete-crack classification pipeline, transformer-based crack classification work, CNN-Transformer segmentation models, and EfficientCrackNet’s tiny segmentation model. The project’s own screened corpus reports multiple published crack classification hybrids and at least 22 crack-specific hybrid segmentation candidates under its stated inclusion rule. Counts depend on whether convolutional tokenization alone qualifies as “hybrid”, and the segmentation count is not a classifier count. **Do not claim first CNN–Transformer crack model, first local/global fusion, first compact hybrid, or first hybrid in crack analysis.**

The defensible boundary is narrower: *a controlled, source-separated study of an explicitly specified very compact binary classifier with one inspectable fusion point, tested against capacity-matched CNN-only, Transformer-only and serial-hybrid controls, and stratified by held-out SDNET surface*. Its potential novelty is experimental specificity and evidence quality, if actually completed—not the general CNN+Transformer recipe. Even this is a contribution claim to qualify against the date-bounded targeted search, not a “first” claim. A direct parallel-branch classifier, EfficientCrackFusion, surfaced in the existing classification screen as a 2026 preprint; its preprint status and internal parameter-count inconsistency warrant inclusion as adjacent prior art, not as a settled peer-reviewed baseline. The project's [classification hybrid inventory](../crack_classification_hybrids.md) and [search-scope statement](../search_scope_and_research_position.md) provide the paper-level screen and its limits.

## Evidence quality risks to report

1. **Unit of split and dependence.** SDNET2018 has 56,092 image tiles from only 230 parent photographs (54 deck, 72 wall, 104 pavement photographs). Tile-level random splitting can put near-neighbour crops, the same parent texture, and acquisition conditions in both train and test. The nominal tile count therefore overstates independent evidence. No source-separated score should be reported until a documented parent-ID mapping and locked group manifest exist. Cluster-aware uncertainty should use parent photographs as the sampling unit where feasible.
2. **Dataset and domain mismatch.** SDNET’s three surfaces differ; wall-only, pavement-only, balanced lab datasets, external bridge samples, and 3D laser segmentation datasets are not interchangeable. Cross-validation on one source pool does not establish external generalization. Leave-one-surface-out results test a useful internal domain shift, not universal real-world transfer.
3. **Class imbalance and metric selection.** SDNET has 8,484 crack versus 47,608 non-crack tiles. Accuracy can be high while crack recall is poor. Report class-sensitive metrics (at least recall, precision/F1, PR-AUC and balanced accuracy), confusion matrices and per-surface results; state the positive class, threshold selection procedure, and whether threshold selection used validation only. Metrics from different datasets, class ratios and thresholds should not be ranked as if they shared a test set.
4. **Training and model fairness.** A pretrained MobileViT-XS is a practical transfer reference, not a causal architecture comparison against scratch-trained compact models. Claiming a fusion benefit requires identical partitions, seeds, augmentation and optimization budget, plus reasonably matched parameter/compute capacity and initialization. Report remaining mismatch, training-selection rule and all seeds. Hyperparameter selection must not use the held-out test/surface.
5. **Efficiency and reproducibility.** Parameter count and FLOPs alone do not establish runtime suitability. Latency and memory depend on hardware, batch size, precision, warm-up, libraries and measurement protocol. Report measured values with the device/software and timing setup; publish split manifests, run configs, checkpoints/predictions or sufficient artifacts to reproduce the estimates.
6. **Review-level limitations.** Broad reviews aggregate studies with different tasks, surfaces, image resolutions, source overlap, splits, pretraining, thresholds and metric definitions. Their claims about common challenges support motivation, not a causal conclusion about the proposed architecture. Scientometric counts depend on database/indexing and search terms; narrative review selection is not exhaustive. The 26 Sep 2026 focused review explicitly calls its 27 papers expert-selected and non-exhaustive. The 2025 Zhang review is a preprint whose dataset/benchmark conclusions should be labeled accordingly.

These points align with the project’s [experimental protocol](../../planning/experimental_protocol.md): current tile-level development results may leak parent photographs and are pipeline checks only. Until verified parent grouping, source-separated training and a sealed test are complete, paper language must say “proposed” or “planned” and must make no performance, generalization, or fusion-benefit claim.

## Interpretability sources and classifier-specific limits

The two primary interpretability references are Selvaraju et al., [Grad-CAM](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCV.2017.74), ICCV 2017, and Abnar & Zuidema, [Quantifying Attention Flow in Transformers](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.385/), ACL 2020. Grad-CAM uses gradients of a target score with respect to a convolutional feature map to form a coarse class-discriminative localization map. It can be applied to a selected convolutional layer in the local branch, but resolution is constrained by that feature map and a highlighted area is not a crack mask or a measurement. Attention rollout/flow aggregate attention across layers to approximate token relevance/flow; they are more informative than raw single-layer attention weights, but remain post-hoc approximations and do not prove causal use of a crack cue. For a binary classifier, explanations should target the crack logit and show true/false positives and negatives across surfaces, including spurious shadows, joints and texture. Treat branch maps as diagnostics and qualitative evidence, not evidence that the model understands structural condition or that attention is inherently explanatory.

## Zotero snapshot coverage audit

The snapshot contains 24 top-level records. It is a metadata export, not a local corpus of attached PDF/full-text content; each record’s `meta.numChildren` signals child items but the snapshot does not include child note text or full-text status. Thus **attachment/child counts cannot independently prove a paper was read in full**. The prior [literature synthesis](scope_existing_literature_synthesis.md) explicitly says all 22 then-current records were read in full and one supplied article was incorporated; the methodology says the original Grad-CAM and attention-flow papers were added as interpretability sources. This is a process note, not an auditable per-item full-text log.

The two interpretability primaries are present as records but have `numChildren: 0` in this snapshot (no attached child PDF/note represented). Seven records have zero children in total: Grad-CAM, Quantifying Attention Flow, Attention Is All You Need, ResNet, ViT, DeiT, and CoAtNet. The other 17 records report one or more child items; only MultiScaleCrackNet, Intelligent Eyes on Buildings, and the 2026 width-estimation review expose an `application/pdf` attachment link in the parent record. Many other records have children without the snapshot identifying whether they are full text, notes, or another attachment. Therefore the accurate cross-check is: **22-record prior full-text claim is documented in the earlier synthesis; the two primary interpretability papers are described as added/read in methodology, but this export does not preserve their full-text notes/PDFs; remaining child status is indeterminate from this JSON alone.** Do not describe these 24 records as individually audited full-text files based solely on the snapshot.

## Claims safe for the paper

- Safe as motivation: review literature repeatedly identifies data scarcity/imbalance, inconsistent evaluation, domain shift/generalization and practical deployment as unresolved concerns.
- Safe as positioning: convolution–Transformer hybrids and crack-specific hybrids already exist; the proposed model is a deliberately small, explicitly ablatable configuration.
- Safe only after completed experiments: source-separated performance, fusion benefit versus matched controls, cross-surface transfer, efficiency, and explanation patterns.
- Not supported by binary crop classification: pixel localization/segmentation, width/depth measurement, severity grading, structural diagnosis, field readiness, safety decisions, or universal generalization.
- Avoid unqualified superlatives (“first”, “state of the art”, “robust”, “real time”, “explainable”) unless a reproducible search, matched comparison, validation protocol, hardware measurement, or faithfulness evaluation specifically supports that term.
