# Image-level crack classification: evidence review

**Scope:** image/patch-level crack presence and severity classification, especially concrete, SDNET2018, and CNN–Transformer classifiers. Detection boxes and pixel masks are outside the primary evidence set.\
**Search date:** 6 October 2026.\
**Coverage:** targeted primary-source search; not a claim of exhaustive coverage. Search-engine access and publisher full-text availability varied. This note records source access explicitly and does not treat abstracts/snippets as equivalent to full-text review.

## Search and screening record

Searches used combinations of: `concrete crack image classification`, `SDNET2018 crack classification`, `CNN Transformer concrete crack classification`, `crack severity image classification`, `MobileViT crack`, `Swin crack classification`, and exact-title/DOI searches for candidate papers. Primary publisher pages and publisher-hosted full-text/PDFs were preferred; the SDNET2018 data paper and relevant open articles were also checked. Screened in papers when the predicted output is an image/patch class (binary crack presence, crack type, or severity). Segmentation/detection-only papers were excluded except where necessary to distinguish task boundaries. Relevant older CNN/classical classifiers were retained as task baselines.

Search log: web searches on 2026-10-06 returned publisher full-text pages or PDFs for SDNET2018, CSW-S, CCTNet, the Swin/EFRB classifier, and the MobileNetV3–LeViT paper; publisher records were accessible for severity classification and the early classical/CNN baseline. Direct requests to some publisher pages were rate-limited (429). Search snippets were used only to locate exact tables/sections, and are not treated as proof of uninspected claims. A true “read every publication” census is not feasible from this targeted search; citation chaining, subscription proceedings, non-English literature, and unpublished work remain coverage gaps.

## High-relevance interpretation

### Dataset and task foundation

SDNET2018 is a patch-classification resource, not 56,092 independent scenes: its 56,092 256×256 tiles derive from 230 parent photographs. The dataset paper presents an AlexNet benchmark and emphasizes variation in widths, shadows, roughness, scaling, edges, holes, and debris. The effective independent source count is therefore far below the tile count. Randomly partitioning tiles without retaining parent identifiers risks sharing capture conditions and nearby surface content between training and test. This is a methodological risk, not proof that any particular prior score is inflated. For this project, source-photo separation is part of the research question and must be described as such; a tile-level development split remains a pipeline check, as stated in [the experimental protocol](../../planning/experimental_protocol.md).

The 2018 Kim et al. study predates SDNET2018 and makes an enduring point for binary classification: cracks must be distinguished from visually similar non-crack noise. SDNET2018 subsequently provides a widely reused binary image/patch benchmark with multiple concrete surface types. These are separate settings and their accuracy values should not be ranked against each other.

### Transformer and hybrid classifiers

CSW-S (2024) and CCTNet (2024) are direct evidence that Transformer-based and CNN–Transformer crack classifiers predate this project. CSW-S is principally a modified Swin classifier with convolutional/residual operations; CCTNet feeds CNN-derived representations into attention. Neither is the same as CrackFusion-Tiny’s explicit small parallel local-convolution and global-attention paths with a single concat-plus-1×1 fusion. This supports a narrowly stated architectural comparison, not a general “first hybrid crack classifier” claim.

The high reported accuracies in CCTNet are authors’ fold results on tiles. Without parent-photo grouping, they do not answer the same generalization question as this project. CSW-S similarly reports high performance on its own 17,000-image setup, but its article does not establish grouped source imagery. Result tables across these studies vary in dataset, sampling, labels, splits, and metric definitions; compare protocols and architecture topology rather than leaderboard numbers.

The 2025 Swin/EFRB paper adds convolution inside/around a hierarchical Transformer. The 2026 Sait et al. paper explicitly stages MobileNetV3 and LeViT and adds Linformer/gated fusion. They are useful precedents for local/global feature combination, but neither is a compact, two-branch model comparable in parameter budget or training conditions to the implemented 0.284M CrackFusion-Tiny. The 2025 severity study extends the task beyond binary labels; its four levels are image categories derived from CCIC and SDNET2018 and should not be represented as physically calibrated severity without checking its full label definition.

The 2026 AttXNet preprint is a useful recent evaluation warning even though its model is CNN-only: the authors use a stratified tile-level split and explicitly state that visually related regions may remain correlated, recommending structure- or inspection-region-level partitions when location metadata exist. Its FPS figure also comes from batch patch processing on a ground workstation in a UAV workflow, not onboard flight hardware. These caveats show why split provenance and timing boundaries must be reported separately from headline scores.

### What this evidence supports for the paper

- State the task as **binary concrete crack image classification**, not crack localization, width measurement, or structural diagnosis.
- Do not claim CNN–Transformer crack classification is new. The defensible distinction is the specific compact parallel topology and the planned source-separated, cross-surface evaluation, if completed.
- Treat SDNET2018 parent photographs as the grouping unit. Report how parent IDs were verified; do not infer provenance from filenames alone.
- Report crack-positive recall/F1 and precision–recall behavior with natural test prevalence, in addition to balanced accuracy and specificity. Accuracy alone is weak evidence on imbalanced SDNET2018.
- Compare architecture results only under matched partitions, preprocessing, training budgets, and seed policy. MobileViT-XS is a pretrained practical reference; scratch-trained matched family baselines answer a different, more controlled architectural question.
- Severity classifiers are relevant related work, but severity labels and crack-presence labels are not interchangeable. A separate severity claim would need defensible label criteria and independent validation.

### Candidate screening after the initial draft

The parent researcher supplied four additional candidates for screening. Özdemir et al. is a direct CNN classifier candidate (attention ConvNeXt), but its abstract-only record does not resolve split provenance. Parvathy & Sivasankar is relevant through MobileViT and explicitly discusses leakage-controlled partitions, though only a non-primary abstract record was available; retain provisionally and verify the original Springer text before relying on it. The UAV inspection arXiv work is a binary CNN classifier, not a CNN–Transformer, so it is included as adjacent deployment evidence and labeled preprint. The suggested CrackNeXt DOI `10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2026.144490` could not be verified. Search instead found *CrackNeXt: An efficient local-to-context hybrid network for concrete surface crack classification in construction material inspection*, DOI `10.1016/j.cscm.2026.e06223`, listed for *Case Studies in Construction Materials* volume 25, **December 2026**, after this survey's 6 October 2026 cutoff; exclude it from the in-scope evidence table despite its close match. Its accessible abstract describes ConvFormer stages followed by Swin stages, METU binary classification and SDNET2018 six-class classification, but it is future-dated relative to this survey cutoff and cannot inform the paper's contemporaneous literature position.

**Unresolved:** exact parent-source grouping in most benchmark papers; full severity-label criteria and KAN-Hybrid topology; comparability of the 2026 mixed-dataset test; a broader citation-chaining and proceedings search. These are explicit limits, not assumed negatives.
