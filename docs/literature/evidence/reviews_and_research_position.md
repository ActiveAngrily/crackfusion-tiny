# Review literature, evidence quality, and paper position

**Cutoff:** 6 October 2026. This is a focused update to the project’s scoping review for the proposed compact concrete-crack image classifier. It is not a PRISMA review or a claim to enumerate every paper in the field.

## Scope and search/screening record

The review question is whether the literature leaves a defensible study contribution for a very small image-level concrete-crack classifier with an explicit local-CNN/global-attention split, assessed under source-photo-separated and cross-surface evaluation. The target is binary classification of a crop/image, not pixel-level crack masks, boxes, physical width, severity, or structural diagnosis.

The project corpus was checked against `sources/zotero_snapshot.json` (24 top-level records). The prior synthesis reports full-text reading of the then-current 22 corpus records, with the two interpretability papers added to the methodology evidence base. Recent survey screening was extended using title/abstract and publisher or author-hosted full text where available. Search concepts included “deep learning crack detection review”, “crack classification concrete review”, “crack generalizability datasets”, “scientometric crack detection”, “CNN Transformer crack classification”, and “3DCrack”. Publisher records, DOI pages, conference proceedings, and author preprints were preferred for title, publication status, scope, and date checks. Segmentation/detection reviews are contextual evidence, not direct classifier comparisons.

The cutoff is applied to actual publication/version dates rather than indexing or crawl dates. Mohammad et al.’s focused Springer review has a version-of-record date of **26 September 2026**, so it is in scope. A Neurocomputing survey surfaced with a publication date of **28 December 2026**; it is post-cutoff and excluded. Zhang et al. (2025) is treated as an arXiv preprint/review plus dataset/benchmark paper, not as a peer-reviewed journal review absent confirmation of later publication. **CrackNeXt (DOI 10.1016/j.cscm.2026.e06223) has an unresolved inclusion date:** the [publisher page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214509526004742) lists a December 2026 issue, while [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/406423686_CrackNeXt_An_Efficient_Local-to-Context_Hybrid_Network_for_Concrete_Surface_Crack_Classification_in_Construction_Material_Inspection) reports June 2026. The publisher’s online-first date could not be accessed to settle whether it was publicly available by the cutoff. **DBD-KD (DOI 10.1016/j.patrec.2026.09.019) also has unresolved cutoff status:** the [publisher page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167865526003363) lists a November 2026 issue, while the September DOI and a [secondary 19 September listing](https://www.ebiotrade.com/newsf/2026-9/20260919001010972.htm) suggest possible earlier online availability; the official online-first date was not verified. Keep both candidates in the prior-art watchlist and do not use them to support a time-bounded novelty claim until their publication dates are settled.

## What the literature establishes—and what it does not

Crack image classification is one resolution of the task. Classification gives a crack/no-crack label for an image or crop; detection predicts a bounding region; segmentation predicts pixels. Measurement requires calibrated geometry or depth, and diagnosis requires contextual structural/material evidence. Review papers often discuss these tasks together under “crack detection”, but their results and metrics are not interchangeable. High segmentation IoU or detector mAP does not validate binary crop classification; a classifier cannot localize a crack or estimate physical width.

The broader architectural idea is established. CNNs provide local, translation-biased representations; ViT and later hybrids combine convolutional processing, tokens, attention, and varied fusion schemes. Crack-specific literature already combines convolution and Transformers, including CCTNet’s concrete-crack classification pipeline, transformer-based crack classification work, CNN-Transformer segmentation models, and EfficientCrackNet’s tiny segmentation model. The project’s screened corpus reports several published crack classification hybrids and at least 22 crack-specific hybrid segmentation candidates under its stated inclusion rule. Counts depend on whether convolutional tokenization alone qualifies as “hybrid”; the segmentation count is not a classifier count. **Do not claim first CNN–Transformer crack model, first local/global fusion, first compact hybrid, or first hybrid in crack analysis.**

The defensible boundary is narrower: *a controlled, source-separated study of a very compact binary classifier with an explicit, inspectable fusion point, tested against capacity-matched CNN-only, Transformer-only, and serial-hybrid controls, and stratified by held-out SDNET surface*. Potential novelty is experimental specificity and evidence quality, if completed—not the general CNN+Transformer recipe. Treat this as a qualified contribution, not a “first” claim. A direct parallel-branch classifier, EfficientCrackFusion, surfaced in the existing classification screen as a 2026 preprint; its preprint status and internal parameter-count inconsistency make it adjacent prior art rather than a settled peer-reviewed baseline. DBD-KD adds frequency/spatial branches to crack-classification distillation, though the accessible snippets do not establish that both branches remain in the inference model. CrackNeXt is a close staged local-to-context classifier. Both papers have unresolved publication dates at the survey cutoff. See the [classification hybrid inventory](../crack_classification_hybrids.md) and [search-scope statement](../search_scope_and_research_position.md) for paper-level screening and limits.

## Evidence quality risks to report

1. **Split unit and dependence.** SDNET2018 has 56,092 tiles from only 230 parent photographs (54 deck, 72 wall, 104 pavement photographs). Tile-level random splitting can place neighbouring crops, parent texture, and acquisition conditions in train and test. Nominal tile count overstates independent evidence. Do not report source-separated scores until a documented parent-ID mapping and locked group manifest exist. Where feasible, estimate uncertainty at the parent-photo sampling unit.
2. **Dataset and domain mismatch.** SDNET’s three surfaces differ; wall-only, pavement-only, balanced lab datasets, external bridge samples, and 3D laser segmentation data are not interchangeable. Cross-validation within one source pool does not establish external generalization. Leave-one-surface-out tests useful internal domain shift, not universal field transfer.
3. **Class imbalance and metrics.** SDNET has 8,484 crack versus 47,608 non-crack tiles. Accuracy can be high while crack recall is poor. Report recall, precision/F1, PR-AUC, balanced accuracy, confusion matrices, per-surface results, positive class, threshold procedure, and validation-only threshold selection. Do not rank metrics from different datasets, class ratios, or thresholds as a shared leaderboard.
4. **Training and model fairness.** Pretrained MobileViT-XS is a practical transfer reference, not a causal architecture comparison against scratch-trained compact models. A fusion-benefit claim requires identical partitions, seeds, augmentation and optimization budget, plus reasonably matched capacity/compute and initialization. Disclose remaining mismatch and selection rules. Never tune on the held-out test/surface.
5. **Efficiency and reproducibility.** Parameters and FLOPs alone do not establish runtime suitability. Latency and memory depend on hardware, batch size, precision, warm-up, libraries, and timing protocol. Report measured values with device/software/setup and provide split manifests, configs, predictions/checkpoints or sufficient reproduction artifacts.
6. **Review-level limitations.** Reviews aggregate tasks, surfaces, resolutions, source overlap, splits, pretraining, thresholds, and metric definitions. Their common-gap claims support motivation, not causal architecture conclusions. Scientometric coverage depends on databases and search terms; narrative selections are not exhaustive. The 26 Sep 2026 focused review explicitly calls its 27 studies expert-selected and non-exhaustive. Zhang et al. is a preprint; CrackNeXt and DBD-KD have unresolved before-cutoff publication dates.

These points align with the project’s [experimental protocol](../../planning/experimental_protocol.md): current tile-level development results may leak parent photographs and are pipeline checks only. Until parent grouping, source-separated training, and a sealed test are complete, use “proposed” or “planned”; make no performance, generalization, or fusion-benefit claim.

## Interpretability sources and classifier-specific limits

The two primary sources are Selvaraju et al., [Grad-CAM](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCV.2017.74), ICCV 2017, and Abnar & Zuidema, [Quantifying Attention Flow in Transformers](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.385/), ACL 2020. Grad-CAM uses gradients of a target score with respect to a convolutional feature map to form a coarse class-discriminative localization map. It can be applied to a selected local-branch convolutional layer, but map resolution is constrained by that feature map and a highlighted area is not a crack mask or measurement. Attention rollout/flow aggregate attention across layers to approximate token relevance/flow. They improve on reading a single raw attention matrix, but remain post-hoc approximations and do not prove causal use of a crack cue. For binary classification, target the crack logit and show true/false positives and negatives across surfaces, including shadows, joints, and texture confounders. Treat branch maps as diagnostics, not proof that the model understands structural condition or that attention is inherently explanatory.

## Zotero snapshot coverage audit

The snapshot contains 24 top-level records. It is a metadata export, not a local corpus of attached full-text content; `meta.numChildren` signals child items but the snapshot omits child note text and full-text status. **Attachment/child counts cannot independently prove a paper was read in full.** The prior [literature synthesis](scope_existing_literature_synthesis.md) says all 22 then-current records were read in full and one supplied article incorporated; the methodology says the original Grad-CAM and attention-flow papers were added as interpretability sources. This records the prior process, not an auditable per-item read log.

Grad-CAM and Quantifying Attention Flow are present but have `numChildren: 0` (no attached child PDF/note represented). Seven records have zero children in total: those two plus Attention Is All You Need, ResNet, ViT, DeiT, and CoAtNet. The other 17 report one or more child items; only MultiScaleCrackNet, Intelligent Eyes on Buildings, and the 2026 width-estimation review expose an `application/pdf` attachment link in the parent record. For other child records, the JSON does not identify whether the child is full text, a note, or another attachment. Accurate conclusion: **the earlier 22-record full-text claim is documented in the synthesis; the two interpretability primaries are described as added/read in methodology, but this snapshot does not preserve their notes/PDFs; remaining child status is indeterminate from this JSON.** Do not claim the 24 records are individually verified full-text files from this export alone.

## Claims safe for the paper

- Safe as motivation: reviews repeatedly identify data scarcity/imbalance, inconsistent evaluation, domain shift/generalization, and practical deployment as unresolved concerns.
- Safe as positioning: convolution–Transformer hybrids and crack-specific hybrids already exist; the proposed model is a deliberately small, explicitly ablatable configuration.
- Safe only after experiments: source-separated performance, fusion benefit versus matched controls, cross-surface transfer, efficiency, and explanation patterns.
- Not supported by binary crop classification: pixel localization/segmentation, width/depth measurement, severity grading, structural diagnosis, field readiness, safety decisions, or universal generalization.
- Avoid “first”, “state of the art”, “robust”, “real time”, and “explainable” unless a reproducible search, matched comparison, validation protocol, hardware measurement, or faithfulness evaluation specifically supports the term.
