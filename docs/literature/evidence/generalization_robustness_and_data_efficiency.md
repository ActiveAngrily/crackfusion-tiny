# Generalization, robustness, and data efficiency

**Survey date:** 6 October 2026\
**Scope:** concrete crack image classification first; adjacent crack detection/segmentation is labeled as analogy. Primary studies only. The survey is targeted, not exhaustive.

## Evidence summary

The strongest direct warning is empirical cross-collection failure: de Almeida et al. report source-only accuracy above 99% on CCiC and about 54% on their Itaipu target test; the target test comprised 118 patches from only three large photographs. This is evidence that within-dataset tile performance can fail to transfer, but not an estimate of broad field performance. Their domain-adaptation result uses unlabeled target data and is a different deployment assumption from zero-shot generalization. [de Almeida et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43503-022-00008-6)

Split independence remains the first practical bottleneck. SDNET2018 contains 56,092 tiles from 230 original photographs (54 deck, 72 wall, 104 pavement); the tile count is not the independent sample count. A 2026 SDNET study explicitly grouped all patches from each source photograph into one partition, while a separate 2026 preprint acknowledges its stratified tile split can retain spatial correlation. The project’s mapping audit still does not have a verified tile-to-parent mapping; until that exists, source-photo-held-out evaluation cannot be claimed. [SDNET source paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2018.11.015); [Zhang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-43534-4); [Li et al., 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27617)

There is direct crack-classification evidence for imbalance and nuisance variation, but little rigorous evidence for corruption robustness or self-supervised gains on independent structures. Recent domain adaptation and semi-supervised papers are mostly dense detection/segmentation, so they motivate experiments without establishing that the same gains hold for a one-tile crack/no-crack classifier. General vision robustness benchmarks are useful protocol analogies only.

## What the evidence says about the avenues

### Source, structure, site, and cross-dataset holdouts

Use nested claims with the held-out unit named. Tile-random estimates additional tiles under the same collection; source-photo holdout estimates unseen photos; structure/inspection/site holdout is the stronger deployment-relevant unit. LOSO over SDNET deck/wall/pavement is a composite domain test: surface type is entangled with structure, capture, prevalence, and potentially location. Report its three rotations individually and do not interpret it as a causal material effect. External data add label-policy and sampling shifts as well as image shift. Deduplicate and inspect provenance before calling a second archive “external”; public crack conglomerates may contain re-packaged public sources.

The most important limitation is independent sample count. When many tiles share a parent photograph, uncertainty intervals and bootstrap resampling must sample parent photos (or structures/sites where metadata allow), not tiles. Multiple random seeds measure optimizer variability, not variation across new structures. With one or a few sites, frame conclusions as case studies.

### Domain adaptation versus domain generalization

Domain adaptation has access to target-domain data, often unlabeled; domain generalization does not. Keep those deployment contracts separate. Direct classification adaptation exists (de Almeida); broader crack adaptation literature is mostly detection or segmentation. First establish a source-only grouped and external baseline. Then test adaptation with a fixed unlabeled target pool and untouched labeled target test set, with no test-time tuning. For domain generalization, leave one source domain out during training/model selection and evaluate the unseen domain once; the current three SDNET surface categories are too few to imply broad unseen-site robustness.

### Distribution shifts and corruptions

Direct concrete crack work identifies illumination, shadows, blur, noise, roughness, stains, and light spots as nuisance conditions; there is not yet a common, source-held-out crack-classification corruption benchmark in this reviewed corpus. A minimal controlled suite should apply predeclared mild/severe blur, sensor noise, exposure/contrast, compression, and occlusion to held-out source groups, report class-sensitive metrics at fixed thresholds, and retain the clean baseline. Synthetic transformations test sensitivity to specified perturbations only; they do not substitute for independently captured adverse-condition images. Avoid arbitrary rotations/flips if those transform crack context or violate deployment orientation assumptions.

### Small sample counts, imbalance, supervision, augmentation

SDNET is imbalanced (8,484 cracked / 47,608 uncracked, about 15.1% positive). A classifier can obtain 84.9% accuracy by predicting only non-crack. Report sensitivity/recall, specificity, PR-AUC, balanced accuracy, and confusion matrix at validation-selected threshold; preserve natural prevalence in test. Compare class-weighted loss or balanced sampling without changing test prevalence. Resampling must occur within training groups to avoid sibling-tile leakage; report calibration/precision because weighting can move the operating point.

Few-shot, self-supervised, and semi-supervised crack papers mostly target segmentation/detection. Their gains do not yet establish data-efficient tile classification. If unlabeled images exist from the deployment environment, an economical test is learning curves at fixed independent-group budgets (e.g. 1/5/10/25/50/100% of labeled training groups), comparing the same compact classifier with standard initialization and in-domain self-supervised pretraining. For semi-supervision, add unlabeled training-domain images only and compare against the same labeled budget. Keep all tiles from each parent together before any augmentation. Augmentation is not a substitute for independent structures; generated variants inherit source-specific texture and may increase apparent n without increasing source diversity.

## Most defensible next experiments

1. **Resolve lineage before benchmarking.** Obtain or build an auditable SDNET tile-to-original-photo map, validate it against original images, and persist the manifest. If impossible, state the limitation and acquire a new grouped dataset; visual nearest-neighbor heuristics alone are not proof of ancestry.
2. **Grouped internal baseline.** Hold out parent photographs, stratify at group level where feasible, maintain natural prevalence in validation/test, and report group counts, tile counts, and class counts. Add group-bootstrap confidence intervals and a validation-only threshold.
3. **Cross-domain evaluation.** Train on grouped SDNET, freeze selection, test each available independent collection with label harmonization and overlap audit. Show per-dataset results and source-only results separately from any target adaptation. CCiC, CCI/CCID, HBC, and Itaipu have different lineage and material constraints; do not pool them as one homogeneous external test.
4. **Structure/site holdout.** Collect multiple structures and preferably repeat capture conditions; reserve complete structures/sites for testing. A lone external site is useful as a stress case but cannot estimate a population-wide generalization rate.
5. **Controlled robustness.** Evaluate a compact, declared corruption grid on grouped test images and collect at least one real adverse-condition set. Predefine severity and report clean-to-corrupt deltas in recall, specificity, PR-AUC, and calibration at a fixed operating threshold.
6. **Data-efficiency ablation.** Use group-count learning curves to compare ordinary supervised initialization, in-domain self-supervised pretraining (if enough unlabeled deployment images exist), and semi-supervision. Match architecture, optimizer budget, and data groups; run at least three seeds, while treating group-bootstrap intervals as the source of generalization uncertainty.
7. **Imbalance check.** Compare plain loss, class weighting, and balanced group-aware sampling on the same grouped split. Keep test prevalence natural and select threshold only on validation; include a majority-class baseline.
