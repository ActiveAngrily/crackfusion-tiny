# Crack datasets, provenance, split leakage, and generalization

**Survey date:** 6 October 2026\
**Scope:** image-level crack/no-crack classification, dataset provenance, split units, domain shift, imbalance, metrics, and evaluation uncertainty. Detection/segmentation datasets are included only where their sampling units or collection design bear on classification. This is a targeted evidence review, not a claim to have found every paper or dataset.

## Executive findings

1. **SDNET2018’s counts and parent-photo total are supported by the dataset paper.** It has 56,092 256×256 tiles from 230 larger images: 54 bridge-deck, 72 wall, and 104 pavement images. The same paper reports 8,484 cracked and 47,608 uncracked tiles. These 230 acquisition images are not necessarily 230 independent bridges, buildings, or pavement sites.
2. **The published parent count does not provide a usable tile-to-parent mapping.** The project audit notebook reports that class folders do not prove tile lineage, leaves `parent_id` blank without a verified supplied map, and marks the current 3,600-image subset as tile-level development only. The source-photo split is therefore a design requirement that remains unfulfilled for the project’s current data artifact.
3. **“Image-level split” is ambiguous.** For pre-cut patch datasets, random splitting the patches is weaker than splitting the original photographs first. For video, temporal, overlapping UAV, or repeated surface views, even a nominal photograph-level split may be weaker than grouping by inspection pass, location, structure, or time series.
4. **Source-photo-held-out, surface-held-out, and external-dataset testing answer different questions.** Leave-one-surface-out (LOSO) is a meaningful composite domain-transfer test, but it does not identify a causal “surface effect”: substrate, location, camera, capture geometry, and class prevalence can shift together. Three surface categories provide only three target domains.
5. **Published cross-dataset evidence is useful but has protocol limits.** A 2025 six-dataset study reports large cross-test drops, but its in-dataset splits are stratified at the patch/image-folder level, not documented as source-grouped; it also reports single-run results. Its cross-domain signal is suggestive, not a controlled estimate of architecture-level domain generalization.
6. **Tile counts exaggerate effective sample size when tiles share a source.** Preserve the natural test distribution, report crack recall/precision and ranking metrics alongside thresholded metrics, select checkpoints and thresholds with validation data only, and base test uncertainty on the source groups—not individual correlated tiles. Seed-to-seed variation and test-sample uncertainty are different quantities.

### SDNET2018 count and mapping verification

The original data article directly gives 230 photographs—54 bridge decks, 72 walls, 104 pavements—and describes more than 56,000 256×256 crops. The class totals are 8,484 cracked and 47,608 uncracked. The 15.1% crack prevalence and 84.9% always-negative accuracy are arithmetic from those counts, not additional reported experimental findings. Surface count cross-checks appear in later SDNET papers, but the dataset article is the preferred source.

Important limit: “230 parent images” is a collection-level fact, not a usable mapping from every released tile to a parent. The local audit notebook explicitly states the six class folders do not prove the parent relation; its rows leave `parent_id` empty absent a documented mapping. Audit output reports 3,600 development images with blank parent IDs and labels them `tile_development_only`. Thus no current local split can be called source-photo-held-out. A parent map recovered from author materials or a traceable extraction manifest is needed; visual similarity clustering can prioritize manual review but must not be asserted as provenance by itself.

## Evaluation units: what each split does and does not test

| Split/evaluation unit | Holdout unit | Valid interpretation | Remaining risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tile-random / patch-random** | Individual released patches | Generalization to more tiles sampled from the same released collection. | Tiles from one photograph can share texture, illumination, crack path, and camera response; overlapping/nearby crops may make test examples close neighbors of training examples. Tile count overstates independent evidence. |
| **Source-photo-held-out** | All tiles from original photographs stay together | Transfer to unseen photographs sampled under broadly similar collection conditions. | Does not prove transfer to unseen structures, sites, devices, or campaigns if several parent photos came from one structure or campaign. Requires complete, auditable lineage. |
| **Surface-held-out / LOSO** | One of deck, wall, pavement excluded from train/validation; tested as a whole | Composite transfer to one unseen SDNET surface category. Report all three held-out surfaces separately. | Surface is confounded with acquisition context and prevalence. With three categories there are only three target domains; score variation cannot isolate a “surface effect.” Parent-photo grouping remains necessary in training/validation surfaces. |
| **External-dataset test** | A separately collected dataset/source held out until final evaluation | Transfer to another collection, acquisition process, and potentially label definition. | Can mix material, camera, crop scale, resolution, selection, class prior and annotation policy. Verify corpus overlap (a dataset may be derived from another), compatible labels, and threshold policy. |
| **Structure/site/inspection-held-out** | Entire bridge/building/site, visit or video sequence | Closest evidence for deployment beyond the specific structures or acquisition pass sampled for training. | Needs metadata and adequate site counts; one external site is a case study, not a broad population guarantee. |

The project protocol proposes three SDNET LOSO rotations. This is a worthwhile evaluation, but phrase it as **held-out-surface transfer** (or a composite domain-shift test), not a controlled test isolating substrate alone. Deck, wall and pavement differ in structural context and collection process as well as surface. A stronger design would pair source-photo grouping within training domains with an independently acquired external/site test, and report its task and labels precisely.

## Direct evidence on source separation and domain shift

### SDNET studies that disclose their split unit

- **Zhang et al. (2026), TinyML pipeline** ([Scientific Reports, doi:10.1038/s41598-026-43534-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-43534-4)) explicitly says it splits at the original high-resolution image level, assigning every patch from a parent only to one of train/validation/test. It samples 16,800 balanced patches from SDNET and uses an 8:1:1 split. This is direct, recent precedent for source-photo grouping. It is not a full-data, natural-prevalence benchmark: it deliberately balances a subset, and the paper’s downstream question is TinyML preprocessing/compression. Reported hardware results are useful separately from generalization claims.
- **Li et al. (2026), AttX-Net** ([arXiv:2604.27617 v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27617), preprint) uses SDNET deck tiles with a stratified 70/15/15 split. Its limitations section explicitly says stratified tile-level splitting may retain correlations among visually similar regions and recommends structure/location/inspection-region splits when metadata exists. It reports 2,025 crack / 11,595 non-crack deck tiles and a validation-selected F1 model. This is a transparent acknowledgement of the exact limitation relevant here, not evidence that the reported tile split estimates structure-held-out performance. The article’s 825 FPS is described for a **ground-station** workflow; despite UAV framing, it does not establish onboard inference.
- **Dorafshan et al. (2018)** provides SDNET counts and collection context, not a parent-ID table or a universal split rule. Later papers’ use of SDNET does not itself establish that all derived samples are source-independent.

### Direct cross-source/cross-domain classification studies

- **de Almeida et al. (2022), “Domain adversarial training for classification of cracking in images of concrete surfaces”** ([AI in Civil Engineering, doi:10.1007/s43503-022-00008-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43503-022-00008-6)) trains on CCiC (40k patches from 458 METU source photos) and tests against images collected at Itaipu Dam. Their target has 294 labelled patches cut from only three high-resolution photos; their described split has 176 train / 118 test patches, and source-only performance averages 54% accuracy on the target while exceeding 99% on the source test. This is unusually direct evidence that high in-domain scores need not transfer. Caveats: target test is only 118 patches from three photos; they also use labelled target patches for target-only/adaptation comparisons and unlabeled target data for adaptation, so distinguish the source-only zero-shot result from adaptation. Their reported accuracy alone does not describe operational precision/recall under natural prevalence.
- **Rashid et al. (2025), “Cross-dataset evaluation of deep learning models for crack classification in structural surfaces”** ([Journal of Municipal Economy, doi:10.1515/jmbm-2025-0074](https://doi.org/10.1515/jmbm-2025-0074)) evaluates CNN, ResNet50, LSTM and VGG16 across SDNET2018, SCD/CCIC, CPC, CDIBM, CCI and HBC. It reports self-test accuracies up to 100% on some datasets and much lower cross-test performance (for example, several models trained on SCD score around 15–26% accuracy on CDIBM). The study describes per-dataset stratified 70/15/15 splits; it does not document source-photo/site-group splitting and uses single runs without seed averaging. It also resizes all datasets to 224×224 and uses ImageNet normalization. Treat the dramatic cross-test pattern as evidence that dataset dependence merits testing, but do not compare its scores directly to this project or treat its self-test as a source-independent estimate. Its discussion attributes differences to resolution, texture, and noise, but those are jointly changed rather than experimentally isolated.
- **Heller et al. (2021), masonry lab-to-field** ([Sensors, doi:10.3390/s21144929](https://doi.org/10.3390/s21144929)) train on controlled brick-wall imagery and test on internet-collected real masonry photographs, observing asymmetric transfer. This is relevant to material/context shift, while its masonry labels/task and acquisition sources differ from SDNET. It reinforces that domain direction matters: A→B and B→A need not be symmetric.

These studies support testing transfer directly. They do not establish one standard “generalization score” for crack classifiers. Source-held-out within SDNET, LOSO, and external-source evaluation should remain separately named results.

## Imbalance, thresholds, metrics, and uncertainty

### Imbalance and prevalence

SDNET2018 has 8,484/56,092 = **15.1%** crack tiles overall, so the majority-class baseline is **84.9% accuracy** with zero crack recall. Surface rates differ: deck 14.9% (2,025/13,620), wall 21.2% (3,851/18,138), pavement 10.7% (2,608/24,334). These rates make accuracy alone inadequate. Keep the final test set at its natural prevalence. If a balanced subset is used for training or development, state that the resulting precision and precision–recall curve do not reproduce field prevalence.

### Metric interpretation

- Report positive-class **crack recall, precision, F1**, confusion matrix and specificity at a declared operating threshold. Recall exposes missed cracks; precision quantifies alert burden. State that “positive” means cracked.
- Report **average precision (AP)** or a precision–recall curve with test prevalence. AP depends on prevalence and is not interchangeable with trapezoidal PR-AUC. Use one name and implementation consistently.
- Report **balanced accuracy** and ROC-AUC as complementary summaries. Neither substitutes for operating-point precision/recall. An ROC-AUC can remain high despite practically low precision when prevalence is low.
- For field decision support, thresholded metrics are meaningful only relative to an operating point. Report calibration or reliability diagnostics (for example Brier score/reliability plot) if probabilities are interpreted as risk; do not claim calibrated risk from classification metrics alone.

### Threshold and model-selection safeguards

1. Predeclare the primary threshold (0.5 is the current protocol default) or select it on a grouped validation set using a named objective and a stated constraint (for example minimum crack recall). Freeze it before the test set is opened.
2. Select checkpoint, hyperparameters, class-weight/sampling strategy, and optional threshold using training/validation only. In LOSO, never use held-out-surface labels or predictions to select epochs, thresholds, augmentations or model variants. A per-surface threshold chosen after viewing each held-out test would erase part of the measured domain shift.
3. Evaluate each held-out surface at the same predeclared operating point, while reporting AP/ROC-AUC for threshold-independent ranking. If a threshold-transfer analysis is desired, label it separately and specify which source validation data established it.
4. Save per-image probabilities and parent IDs before test scoring. Report metric code/version and resolve the project’s documented threshold-call bug before extending the current trainer to a threshold study; current audit says completed run used 0.5 and was unaffected.

### Uncertainty: sources are clusters, seeds are not datasets

Tiles within one source photo are clustered. A tile bootstrap would treat nearby tiles as independent and generally understate uncertainty. For a source-photo test, bootstrap **parent photos**, carrying every tile and all model predictions for each sampled photo together. For architecture comparisons, use a paired cluster bootstrap: resample the same parent IDs for both models and compute the metric difference in each replicate. If the deployment target is new structures or campaigns, the relevant higher-level cluster is site/structure/campaign, not the tile or possibly even the photo.

With only 230 documented SDNET source photographs and three surface types, intervals should not be interpreted as guarantees for all concrete infrastructure. Unequal numbers of tiles per photo also mean a tile-weighted metric and a photo-weighted metric answer different questions; make the target estimand explicit. Three fixed training seeds estimate optimization variation conditional on the same data/split. Their mean and standard deviation are **not** a confidence interval over new source photos or structures. Report seed-level results separately from a source-cluster uncertainty interval. With few top-level sites, interval methods can themselves be unstable; show group counts and per-group/per-surface distributions rather than presenting a narrow interval as certainty.

General clustered-data reference: Field & Welsh, “Bootstrapping clustered data,” *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society B* 69(3), 369–390 (2007), [doi:10.1111/j.1467-9868.2007.00593.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2007.00593.x). The principle is to reproduce the dependence structure by resampling independent clusters, not lower-level observations. This is statistical-method guidance, not crack-specific evidence.

## Recent dataset additions and task boundaries

- **Attain (2025)** adds smartphone/video-derived road-distress images with expert instance annotations, several distress classes, and severity. Its temporal acquisition suggests grouping by source video/drive. It is relevant to future pavement distress and operational evaluation; its objects and scene imagery are not a drop-in SDNET binary classification test.
- **PaveTrack (2025)** explicitly separates detection images from repeated-defect tracking imagery. Its temporal component is useful for study designs where the test unit is a tracked defect/site. The detection split alone should not be called source-held-out absent an explicit source-unit statement.
- **Historical-Crack18-19** demonstrates a steep “tiles vs. sources” ratio: 3,886 patches come from around 40 photographs at one historic mosque. It is useful for illustrating why view-level split counts need parent lineage; it is not broad evidence across historic structures.
- **CRACK-CH** brings historic stone/masonry contexts but remains a different material and likely a few-site collection; group by site and inspect the actual version.
- **StructDamage (2026 preprint)** may broaden the source roster by merging dozens of prior datasets. Its large nominal image count cannot establish independent source diversity. Before external evaluation, audit whether SDNET, CCIC, or derivatives are included, how duplicates and parent lineage are handled, and whether folds partition at the underlying source dataset/site level.

Dataset labels and names are not interchangeable: “CCIC” generally names the 40k METU-cropped patch set, while “CCI/CCID” here names a separate Gümüşhane smartphone collection whose published counts differ by version/source. Likewise “HBC” can mean a historically collected patch dataset from one building, not a representative sample of historic buildings.

## Implications for CrackFusion-Tiny

The scientific question in the current protocol—whether an explicit lightweight local/global fusion helps under source-separated and cross-surface evaluation—is defensible if the results honor the split units and do not imply universal field readiness.

**Required before final-study claims:**

1. Establish a complete, documented SDNET tile→source-photo map; preserve the mapping source and audit coverage. If this cannot be recovered, keep the existing tile subset explicitly as pipeline development and do not call it source-separated.
2. Split groups before assigning train/validation/test. Keep every parent photo in one partition and exact duplicates across one split at most. For held-out-surface rotations, make grouped train/validation splits from the two training surfaces and keep the full third surface sealed for final scoring.
3. Freeze architecture, hyperparameters, checkpoint rule and threshold using validation only. Use the held-out surface exclusively for final evaluation; disclose that LOSO shifts several factors together.
4. Preserve natural class proportions in the final test. Report crack recall, precision, F1, AP (not mislabeled PR-AUC), balanced accuracy, specificity, ROC-AUC, confusion matrix and per-surface counts. Include all three seeds and distinguish seed spread from cluster uncertainty.
5. Use source-photo-clustered paired uncertainty for within-SDNET model comparisons. Treat only three LOSO targets as three domain cases. A separate, provenance-checked external dataset or site is stronger evidence for external transfer, but it must have comparable label semantics and no source overlap.
6. Avoid scope creep: Attain/PaveTrack/CRACK-CH and segmentation/severity collections are future datasets unless the paper’s task changes. Their count scale does not make them compatible binary tests.

## Evidence boundary

This note establishes a source-backed design rationale and flags important dataset lineage issues. It does not audit every crack benchmark, download and checksum every external archive, establish every archive’s license, or prove a quantitative inflation factor for tile leakage. The existence of correlated tiles makes ordinary tile-random estimates potentially optimistic; the size of any bias must be measured with matched splits. Treat preprints and new composite datasets as provisional until data, code, source overlap, and split manifests can be independently inspected.
